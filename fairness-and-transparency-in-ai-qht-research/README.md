# ⚖️ Research Café: AI, Law & Technology  
*April – June 2025 | University of Westminster*

## 📌 Project Overview  
As part of the Quintin Hogg Trust-funded Research Café programme, I collaborated with fellow Computer Science and Law students to explore bias and transparency in AI — particularly in healthcare applications like cancer diagnosis. This interdisciplinary project combined technical, legal, and ethical perspectives to better understand the responsibilities of those building intelligent systems.

## 🧰 Tools & Techniques  
- **Fairlearn** – for exploring bias mitigation through reweighting and fairness-aware evaluation  
- **SHAP** – for model explainability and interpreting feature contributions  
- **GDPR & Legal Frameworks** – including consent control, bias audits, and algorithmic accountability  
- **Explainable AI (XAI)** – methods to ensure model decisions are transparent and traceable  
- **Bias Mitigation Strategies** – including diverse data sampling and weighting techniques

## 🧠 What I Worked On  
- Analysed how bias manifests in machine learning pipelines  
- Explored fairness techniques like dataset weighting and inclusive feature engineering  
- Studied methods for building **explainable AI**, including model transparency and interpretability  
- Learned about legal frameworks like **GDPR**, **bias audits**, and **consent controls**  
- Contributed to group research discussions and insight-sharing sessions

## 🤝 Team & Collaboration  
I had the pleasure of working with Mahnoor, Daniella, and Apurva — three brilliant and inspiring women. Our different academic backgrounds helped create a rich, well-rounded perspective on the societal impacts of AI.

## 💬 What I Learned  
This project made me reflect on my role as a data scientist — not just to build accurate models, but to build **fair and explainable ones**. It reminded me that algorithms can unintentionally reinforce inequality if we don’t take active steps to question our data, understand our model decisions, and stay informed about the legal and human impact of our work.

---

> “AI isn’t neutral — it reflects the choices we make when we build it. This project helped me take responsibility for those choices.”
